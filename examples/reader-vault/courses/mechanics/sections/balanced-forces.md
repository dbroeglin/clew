# Balanced forces

<!-- clew:nav -->
Course: [[courses/mechanics/course|Mechanics of everyday motion]] · Chapter: [[courses/mechanics/chapters/forces|Explaining motion]]
Previous: [[courses/mechanics/sections/speed-from-a-graph|Speed from a distance-time graph]] · Next: none
Originals: [[courses/mechanics/sources/mechanics-notes.pdf#page=3|mechanics-notes.pdf, page 3]]
<!-- /clew:nav -->

The everyday intuition is that keeping something moving requires a continuous
push. On a level surface without friction it does not. What a push changes is
the velocity, not the motion itself.

Forces add as vectors, so the net force on an object is

$$
\vec F_{\text{net}} = \sum_i \vec F_i .
$$

When $\vec F_{\text{net}} = \vec 0$ the forces are balanced and the velocity
does not change: an object at rest stays at rest, and one already moving keeps
the same speed in the same direction.

## Why the intuition feels right

A box pushed across a floor stops because friction is there, not because motion
needs a supply of force. The push is balancing friction rather than sustaining
the motion. Remove the friction and the same push would keep speeding the box
up.

## What a net force does

An unbalanced force changes velocity in proportion to itself and in inverse
proportion to the mass:

$$
\vec a = \frac{\vec F_{\text{net}}}{m}.
$$

Acceleration is a change of velocity, which includes a change of direction.
Something moving at a constant speed around a circle is accelerating, because
its direction keeps changing.
