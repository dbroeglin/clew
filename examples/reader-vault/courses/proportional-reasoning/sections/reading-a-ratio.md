# Reading a ratio

A [[concepts/math-ratios|ratio]] compares two quantities by dividing one by the
other. Writing $a : b$ for the comparison of $a$ with $b$, the ratio is the
single number

$$
r = \frac{a}{b}, \qquad b \neq 0 .
$$

A recipe using $200\ \mathrm{g}$ of flour for $100\ \mathrm{ml}$ of milk has a
flour-to-milk ratio of $2$, whatever quantity is actually made.

## Equivalent ratios

Multiplying both quantities by the same non-zero number leaves the ratio
unchanged:

$$
\frac{ka}{kb} = \frac{a}{b}, \qquad k \neq 0 .
$$

So $200 : 100$, $400 : 200$ and $2 : 1$ are the same ratio written three ways.
This is the property every later use depends on.

## Ratios and fractions

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind, and the result has no unit.
Dividing quantities of different kinds gives a rate instead, such as the
distance-over-time of an average speed.

$$
\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ ka & kb \end{bmatrix}
$$

Both rows above describe the same ratio, which is what makes the table a
legitimate way to record one.
